CLASS 2 · TEXT THAT MEANS SOMETHING

Choose words—and the right elements.

Headings, paragraphs, and text elements help readers understand what matters, what is quoted, and how parts of a page relate.

A SMALL BUSINESS EXAMPLE

Abebe's Café

The best macchiato in Piassa, served with care since 1998.

Open daily
7:00–21:00

© 2026 Abebe's Café & Bakery

This page uses paragraphs for related text and a line break for the address-like opening hours. The horizontal rule marks a change of topic.

Formatting carries meaning

Water is H2O. The area of a circle is πr2.

Regular price: 500 Birr; today's price: 350 Birr.

Important: the exam is tomorrow.

Highlighted text draws attention to a phrase in context.

Bold and italic describe appearance or an alternate voice; strong and emphasis communicate importance or stress.

Quotes, abbreviations, and titles

WHO is an abbreviation. A short inline quotation uses the q element: its goal is health for all.

Education is the most powerful weapon which you can use to change the world.

Long Walk to Freedom is a book title. Use cite for the title of a creative work, not the author's name.

Reserved characters and entities

To show markup as text, write &lt; for < and &gt; for >. Use &amp; when you need an ampersand, and &copy; for ©.

UTF-8 lets you write many characters directly, including € and ሰላም.
